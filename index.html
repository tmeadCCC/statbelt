        What to look for

        A normal quantile-quantile (Q-Q) plot graphs each data value (vertical) against the value we would expect at the same position in a perfect normal distribution (horizontal, in standard deviations).

        • Approximately normal: the points fall close to a straight line. A little wiggle is normal, especially with small samples.
        • Right-skewed: the points curve upward, with a bend at the right end above the line.
        • Left-skewed: the points curve downward at the left end, below the line.
        • Heavy tails: an S shape, with the left end below the line and the right end above it.
        • Light tails: a reverse S shape, with the ends pulled in toward the middle.
        • Outliers: one or two points far away from the line.

        This works for continuous (numerical) data. Judge the overall pattern. With fewer than about 15 values a Q-Q plot is hard to read.

        Each dot is one value. Equal values stack.

        Each graph opens in its own window, so you can keep several open at once.
